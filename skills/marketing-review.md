# Marketing review

Review existing material — README, landing page, launch post, email, ad, docs
homepage — and say what to fix, in priority order. You do not edit the files;
the review is the deliverable.

## Read it as the reader

First pass: read only what a visitor sees in the first five seconds (headline,
subhead, first screen or first paragraph). Write down what you believe the
product is, who it is for and what to do next. If any of the three is unclear,
that is finding #1.

## Checklist

1. **Clarity** — what it is, for whom, why it matters, all above the fold.
2. **Positioning** — is there a clear alternative it beats? Could a competitor
   use the same words?
3. **Audience and awareness stage** — does the opening match how much the
   reader already knows?
4. **Benefits vs. features** — do features carry a "which means"?
5. **Proof** — demo, screenshot/GIF, numbers, real testimonials, adoption
   signals. Is every claim backed?
6. **Objections** — are price, trust, switching cost, "why not build it
   myself" answered?
7. **CTA** — one primary action, visible, outcome-worded, repeated at the end.
8. **Friction** — install or signup steps, required fields, time to first
   value. Count the steps.
9. **Voice and consistency** — one voice, consistent terms, no jargon the ICP
   would not use.
10. **Compliance** — run `compliance-de-eu.md` and include its table.
11. **Search and AI visibility** — title/description, heading structure,
    crawlable text, AI crawler policy, quotable claims (see
    `search-visibility.md`).
12. **Accessibility** — alt text, link text, contrast, no copy only in images.
13. **Authenticity** — does it read as generic AI copy? (`copywriting.md`,
    anti-slop pass.)

## Report

- **Gate** — one of:
  - `SHIP` — works; fixes are optional polish.
  - `FIX` — ship after the listed fixes.
  - `BLOCK` — must not go out: a compliance `block`, a false claim, or a
    reader cannot tell what the product is.
  - `UNDECIDED` — needs information you do not have; say which.
- **Verdict** — one paragraph: does it work, and the single biggest problem.
- **Findings** — ordered by impact, each with: what is wrong (quote the
  text), why it costs conversions or trust, the concrete fix — rewritten copy
  where the fix is copy.
- **What already works** — keep it, so nobody "improves" it away.
- **Claims check** — every factual claim found, with proof or `[NEEDS PROOF]`.

Severity is about the reader's decision, not about taste. Do not list
stylistic preferences as findings.
