# Copywriting

Write copy that makes one reader take one action. Start from the messaging
house (see positioning-messaging); if none exists, build a minimal one first
and say so.

## Before writing

- **Reader**: who, at what awareness stage — unaware, problem-aware,
  solution-aware, product-aware, most-aware. The stage decides the opening: the
  less aware, the more you lead with the problem; the more aware, the faster
  you get to the offer.
- **One action**: star, install, sign up, reply, buy. One page, one primary CTA.
- **Channel constraints**: length, format, where it is read (GitHub README,
  mobile inbox, X feed, HN title, App Store).

## Frameworks — pick by situation, not by habit

| Situation | Framework |
|---|---|
| Landing page, README top | Headline → subhead → proof → benefits → how it works → objections/FAQ → CTA |
| Pain is well known | PAS: Problem → Agitate → Solution |
| Short ad, cold email | AIDA: Attention → Interest → Desire → Action |
| Feature to benefit | Feature → "which means" → outcome |
| Before/after story | BAB: Before → After → Bridge |
| Launch post (HN, Reddit, dev communities) | Plain: what it is, why you built it, what is different, what is rough. No hype — technical audiences punish it |

## Craft rules

- Headline does the work: clear first, clever only if it stays clear. Test:
  does a stranger know what it is and who it is for in five seconds?
- Benefits lead, features prove.
- Concrete over abstract: "Resumes a Claude session in Codex after a rate
  limit" beats "seamless multi-model workflows".
- Short sentences. Active voice. Cut every word that does not change meaning.
- CTA names the outcome ("Start routing your agents"), not the mechanism
  ("Submit").
- Match the brand's voice; if none is documented, propose one in three
  adjectives with a do/don't example each.
- German copy: decide du vs. Sie up front and keep it consistent; avoid
  Denglisch where a clear German word exists, keep established tech terms.

## Always deliver variants

For each headline, subject line, CTA or ad: at least three variants built on
different angles (pain, outcome, curiosity, proof, identity), each with one line
of rationale, and your pick. Variants that differ only in wording are one
variant.

## Claims and compliance check — run before handing over

- **No fabrication.** No invented numbers, testimonials, user counts, logos,
  awards or reviews. Use `[TESTIMONIAL: needed from a real user]`-style
  placeholders.
- **Substantiation.** Superlatives and comparisons ("fastest", "#1", "better
  than X") need evidence you can cite. Under German law (UWG §5, §6) misleading
  or unprovable comparative claims are actionable; under the FTC Act the same
  holds in the US. Flag every such claim with its proof or remove it.
- **Email and messaging.** Marketing email needs prior consent; in Germany
  double opt-in is the practical standard (DSGVO, UWG §7). Every mail needs an
  unsubscribe link and the sender's identity (Impressum).
- **Ads and influencers.** Paid or affiliate content is labelled as such
  ("Anzeige", "#ad").
- **No dark patterns.** No fake countdowns, fake scarcity, confirmshaming or
  pre-ticked boxes.
- **Accessibility.** Link text says where it goes; images get alt text; never
  put essential copy only inside an image.

List the result of this check in the report: claims made, proof for each,
anything flagged.
