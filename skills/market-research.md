# Market and competitor research

Find out what the market looks like from evidence, with every claim traceable.
The same provenance rules as a researcher apply: primary sources first, search
snippets are leads, model memory is not a source.

## What every claim carries

- Source precise enough to check (URL, page, date of the post, version).
- Retrieval date — pricing pages and feature lists move.
- Confidence and what would change it.

Keep **verified facts** and **inferences** in separate sections.

## Competitor teardown

For each competitor (including the "do nothing / build it yourself"
alternative):

| Dimension | Where to look |
|---|---|
| One-liner and category they claim | Homepage hero, meta description, GitHub description |
| ICP they target | Case studies, testimonials, pricing tiers, job ads |
| Pricing and packaging | Pricing page — note free tier, limits, the tier they push |
| Key claims and proof | Homepage, comparisons, docs |
| Channels | Blog, social presence, SEO footprint (which queries they rank for), communities, ads |
| Traction signals | GitHub stars/commit activity, npm/PyPI downloads, release cadence, public user counts — label the source |
| What users complain about | GitHub issues, Reddit, HN comments, G2/Capterra, app store reviews |

Then: where are they strong, where are they weak, and which gap matches what
our product actually does. The complaints column is usually where the
positioning comes from.

## Customer and market evidence

- **Voice of customer**: mine issues, forums, reviews and HN/Reddit threads for
  the exact words people use about the problem. Quote them with links. These
  feed headlines.
- **Market size**: only when asked, and bottom-up (number of reachable
  customers × realistic price) rather than a top-down analyst figure. Show the
  arithmetic and every assumption.
- **Trends**: search interest, community growth, adjacent launches — each with
  a source and date.

## Interview and survey design (when asked)

- Ask about past behaviour, not hypothetical future intent ("When did you last
  hit this? What did you do?"), never "Would you use…?".
- Five to eight interviews per segment usually shows the pattern.
- Surveys: one question per idea, no leading wording, include "other" and
  "none".

## Report

- **Answer** — the finding, first.
- **Competitor table** — as above, sourced.
- **Gaps and opportunities** — what follows for positioning, pricing, channels.
- **Uncertain** — what you could not establish and how to settle it.
- **Sources consulted** — including dead ends.
