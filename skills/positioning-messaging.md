# Positioning and messaging

Positioning decides who the product is for and why it wins for them. Messaging
is how that gets said. Do positioning first; copy written without it is
decoration.

## Inputs to collect before writing anything

Read the README, docs, landing page, changelog and issues. Look for what the
product actually does, not what it says about itself. If customer evidence
exists (issues, reviews, support threads, interview notes), read it — users'
own words beat yours.

## Procedure

1. **Competitive alternatives.** What would the customer do if this product
   did not exist? Usually not a competitor: a spreadsheet, a script, an intern,
   doing nothing. List 3–5.
2. **Unique attributes.** What does the product have that those alternatives
   lack? Only verifiable features or properties.
3. **Value.** For each attribute: "so what?" until you hit an outcome the
   customer cares about (time, money, risk, status, peace of mind). Drop
   attributes that lead nowhere.
4. **Best-fit customer (ICP).** Who cares most about that value? Be concrete:
   role, company or situation, trigger event, what they already tried. "Developers"
   is not an ICP; "solo devs running several AI coding CLIs who hit rate
   limits weekly" is.
5. **Jobs to be done.** For the ICP: "When <situation>, I want to <motivation>,
   so I can <outcome>." Include the functional, emotional and social job.
6. **Market category.** The frame that makes the value obvious. Pick an
   existing category if the product wins in it; a new category only if no
   existing frame fits — creating one is expensive.
7. **Pains and objections.** What makes the ICP hesitate: switching cost,
   trust, price, "I can build this myself". Each needs an answer in the
   messaging.

## Output: the messaging house

- **Positioning statement** (internal, not copy): For <ICP> who <need>,
  <product> is a <category> that <key value>. Unlike <main alternative>, it
  <decisive difference>.
- **One-liner** — under 12 words, says what it is and for whom.
- **Value proposition** — headline + one supporting sentence.
- **3 pillars** — each a benefit, with its proof points (feature, number,
  demo, quote) listed underneath. A pillar with no proof is a hope; mark it.
- **Objection handling** — objection → answer → proof.
- **Words to use / words to avoid** — taken from customer language where you
  have it.

## Quality bar

- Could a competitor paste your one-liner onto their site unchanged? Then it is
  not positioning. Rewrite.
- Every claim traces to a proof point or is marked `[NEEDS PROOF]`.
- Specific beats clever. Numbers beat adjectives. The customer's words beat
  yours.
- If evidence for the ICP is thin, give two or three candidate positionings and
  say what test would decide between them.
