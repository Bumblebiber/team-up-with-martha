# Go-to-market and launch

Plan how the product reaches its ICP: which channels, in what order, with what
offer, and what counts as success. You plan; a human ships.

## Choose the motion first

| Motion | Fits when |
|---|---|
| Product-led (free tier, self-serve, open source) | Low price, fast time-to-value, users can try it alone — most dev tools |
| Community-led | The audience gathers somewhere (Discord, subreddits, GitHub) and the product benefits from sharing |
| Content/SEO-led | People actively search for the problem; long horizon, compounding |
| Sales-led | High price, several stakeholders, procurement |

Say which motion and why. Most early products need one primary channel done
well, not seven done thinly.

## Channel selection

List candidate channels, then score each: reach to the ICP, cost (money and
hours), time to first signal, fit with the motion. Pick one primary, one or two
secondary. Typical channels for developer tools: GitHub README and topics, Hacker
News (Show HN), relevant subreddits, X/Bluesky/Mastodon dev communities, Product
Hunt, dev.to / Hashnode, newsletters (sponsorship or submission), awesome-lists,
package registries, YouTube demos, Discord/Slack communities, integrations and
marketplaces (MCP registries, plugin directories).

Check each community's self-promotion rules before recommending it, and cite
them.

## Pricing and packaging (input, not decision)

- Anchor on value delivered and on the alternatives' prices, not on cost.
- Value metric: what the customer pays per (seat, run, project, usage). It
  should grow with the value they get.
- Good-better-best tiers; the middle tier is the one you want chosen.
- Open source: decide what is free forever, what is paid (hosted, team,
  support), and say it plainly — ambiguity reads as bait-and-switch.

## Launch plan

1. **Readiness** — positioning and one-liner final; README/landing page,
   demo (GIF or 60–90 s video), install path tested from zero, docs for the
   first use case, a way to collect feedback, analytics on the conversion
   events.
2. **Pre-launch** — warm the channels: early users, a waitlist or beta, people
   who will genuinely try it on day one. No vote rings — they break platform
   rules and get posts killed.
3. **Launch day** — per channel: the exact asset (title, post, first comment),
   posting time in the channel's timezone, who answers comments for the first
   hours.
4. **Post-launch** — follow-up content, changelog posts, collecting the
   testimonials you were not allowed to invent, second-wave channels.

## Output

A launch brief: motion, ICP, one-liner, primary and secondary channels with
rationale, the asset list per channel (with drafts where asked), a timeline,
success metrics with targets, and risks. Mark every step a human must perform.
